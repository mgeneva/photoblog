I once spent a week at the ski report of Rusutsu in Hokkaido with my sister.

Why?  I hadn’t skied in nearly a decade. As a child, skiing was a regular winter activity, but that stopped once we moved somewhere far from the mountains. Years passed without touching the slopes. Then, I joined an impromptu weekend ski trip to Hokkaido as an adult. The snow was incredible, and to my surprise, skiing came back easily. That short trip planted the idea: I wanted to return and do it properly.

This time, we stayed for a full week at Rusutsu, and it was so memorable.  The stay itself brought a multitude of small, quiet joys. Each morning, we redeemed our breakfast coupons for a Japanese-style meal, including a dish I discovered on this trip: annin tofu with kuroame. Passing by the German-style merry-go-round on our way to and from our room became a simple ritual that grounded us each day.

![German Merry Go Round]({{ "" | relative_url }}/assets/images/Hokkaido1.JPG)

What made the week especially meaningful, though, was the group lesson we took. Our experienced instructor seemed genuinely surprised at how quickly we picked up and applied his guidance. He introduced us to powder skiing, something I’ll never forget. The skiing sensation was entirely new, like slicing a knife through smooth butter.

By the end of the lessons, his advice stayed with me: keep exploring different terrain, keep learning, and enjoy the process of gaining new experiences.

![Rusutsu Slopes]({{ "" | relative_url }}/assets/images/Hokkaido2.JPG)

That week in Hokkaido gave me more than just good memories - it gave me confidence. Not long after, I went on a ski trip to Vermont. The conditions couldn’t have been more different: hard, icy, and far less forgiving. But because I had reconnected with skiing on Hokkaido’s soft and light snow, I managed.  More than that, it pushed me to keep going.
