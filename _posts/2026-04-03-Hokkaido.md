As a child, I went skiing almost every week, but that stopped once we moved far from the mountains. A decade passed without me touching the slopes. Then, as an adult, I had to return to Japan for a short emergency trip, and I joined my family for a quick weekend ski getaway in Hokkaido. The snow was incredible, and to my surprise, skiing came back easily. That short trip planted the idea: I wanted to return and do it properly.

That's why a few years later, I went back to Hokkaido with my sister, and we stayed for a full week at the ski resort of Rusutsu.  It was a memorable trip, filled with a multitude of small, quiet joys. Each morning, we redeemed our breakfast coupons for a Japanese-style meal, including a dish I discovered on this trip: annin tofu with kuroame. Passing by the German-style merry-go-round on our way to and from our room became a simple ritual that grounded us each day.

![German Merry Go Round]({{ "" | relative_url }}/assets/images/Hokkaido1.jpg)

What made the week especially meaningful though was the ski lesson we had with our wonderful instructor. He pushed us to do new things and seemed genuinely surprised at how quickly we picked up and applied his guidance. He introduced us to powder skiing, something I’ll never forget. The sensation was entirely new, like slicing a knife through smooth butter.

By the end of the lessons, his advice stayed with me: keep exploring different terrain, keep learning, and enjoy the process of gaining new experiences.

![Rusutsu Slopes]({{ "" | relative_url }}/assets/images/Hokkaido2.jpg)

That week in Hokkaido gave me more than just good memories - it gave me confidence. Not long after, I went on a ski trip to Vermont. The conditions couldn’t have been more different: hard, icy, and far less forgiving. But because I had reconnected with skiing on Hokkaido’s soft and light snow, I was able to handle those conditions.  More than that, my Hokkaido experiences encouraged me to keep going.
