I took a solo trip to Peru before the pandemic. The highlight came after I flew to Cusco following five days of language school in Lima.

My time there was short but so memorable. On my first full day, I wandered through the cobblestone streets of Cusco and made my way up to Sacsayhuamán, climbing what felt like endless stairs before being rewarded with breathtaking views.  The next day, I boarded a train to visit Machu Picchu — an experience that was magical from start to finish. On my final full day, I joined a group tour through the Sacred Valley, taking in different landscapes and sights such as the Pisac ruins, Ollantaytambo, and Chinchero.

![Exploring the city of Cusco and Sacsayhuamán]({{ "" | relative_url }}/assets/images/Cusco1.jpg)

What stays with me most, though, are the small, vivid moments in between.

At my very first meal, I watched two French women confidently order cuy (guinea pig). In Cusco’s cathedral, I found myself captivated by the artwork—especially a depiction of the Last Supper that featured guinea pig on the table and chicha morada being served, with alpacas appearing in place of camels in other frescoes.  I also enjoyed in Cusco one of the most beautiful salads I’ve ever had in my life.
{% include gallery id="gallery1" %}

At Machu Picchu, I was fascinated to learn that the site may have served as a kind of ancient scientific research center. The precision of the stonework and the intentionality behind every structure left me in awe.  I felt so enchanted standing among the ruins that I got overexcited and  stamped my actual passport with a Machu Picchu stamp. 

And then there was my final evening in Cusco: reuniting with friends from language school, sitting together on a balcony perched along the slopes of the city, sharing drinks and talking as we watched the lights flicker on across the surrounding mountains.
